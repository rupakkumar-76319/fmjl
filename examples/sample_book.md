# CHAPTER I.

<!-- e8 page=3 bbox=122,157,878,354 -->
In the autumn of that year Mara climbed the tower before dawn, as she had done for eleven years, and checked the lamp with the slow care her father had taught her. The sea below was grey and patient, and the gulls had not yet woken. She wrote the hour in the margin, the state of the wind, and a line about the boats that had passed in the night, because a keeper who forgets the small things will one day forget a large one. When the light was steady she sat on the top step and watched the coast take shape out of the dark, first the rocks, then the harbour wall, then the roofs of the village where nobody remembered that the tower had a keeper at all.
